The kit — what to actually bring

One of each. Everything below fits in one cabin bag between you.

Jackets — two, not one

Winter jacket: a packable down or synthetic puffer, the kind that squashes into its own pocket. That is your warm layer for Lenox evenings, Vermont mornings and both summits. Not a heavy wool overcoat — too bulky, not warm enough in wind, useless when wet.

Normal jacket: whatever you'd wear on a cool evening in Ahmedabad in January — denim, a light bomber, a blazer. New York only. Optional; the fleece does the same job.

Rain shell: a thin waterproof with a hood. Goes over everything on the summits as a windbreak, and over the fleece if it rains. Weighs nothing.

Shoes — two pairs

Waterproof walking boots or shoes: Kaaterskill is wet rock and spray, Storm King is wet grass, both summits are mud and wind. Ankle support is nice, waterproof is essential. Break them in at home for a week first — new boots on a 5 km trail is how holidays go wrong.

Comfortable sneakers: for New York, Lenox village and the drive. You will walk 10–12 km a day in Manhattan; cushioning matters more than looks.

Leave at home: leather dress shoes, sandals, anything canvas.

Thermals — two days only

Greylock (Thu 22) and Equinox (Sat 24) are the thermal days — both over 1,000 m, both near freezing with wind. One set of thermal top and bottoms, merino or synthetic; put them on at the hotel in the morning, take them off at lunch.

Everywhere else a long sleeve under the fleece is enough. The fleece itself is the hardest-working thing in the bag: on every day from the 19th to the 26th.

Small things that matter: a warm hat that covers the ears, thin gloves (touchscreen-friendly for photos), wool socks for the boot days, sunglasses — October sun is low and straight in your eyes on the drive.
